## Key Stories
- **Two decades of borrowed leadership**: Working in learning and development, Joanna saw that her successful colleagues were all big-personality extroverts. She used them as a blueprint to be "less like myself." It worked until burnout and age made her stop compromising who she is.
- **The training room in Doha**: Joanna was helping Qatari nationals work together as a team. She saw what she had seen all over the world: the quiet people doing great work were consistently overlooked. She got mad, and that anger became her mission.
- **"The last acceptable bias"**: A director of communications told her that extroversion bias "seems" to be the last acceptable bias. The line hit her hard and still shapes her work.
- **Credibility vs. visibility with Richard**: Joanna and a colleague were designing an influencing-skills game. He assumed visibility comes first and credibility second, and she was "aghast." That disagreement became her credibility-then-visibility model.
- **The oversubscribed lunch and learn**: At a professional services firm, a senior leader joked, "good luck getting the introverts to turn up." Her session became the most oversubscribed one the firm had ever run.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Comfort Zone → Competency Zone → Learning Edge**: Joanna rejects "nothing good happens in your comfort zone." The comfort zone is where you are unconsciously competent. Just beyond it is the competency zone, where you have to concentrate to perform. Beyond that is the learning edge: mildly uncomfortable, but not fear or panic. Working at the learning edge grows your comfort zone sustainably.
- **Credibility, Then Visibility**: First build trust and expertise and give yourself time to prepare. Then step into the limelight, lead the meeting, or present the paper. Introverts who want to go visibility-first still can.
- **Reframe "You're Quiet" as a Strength**: Instead of apologizing, say: "Thank you for noticing, because one of my real strengths is my ability to observe what's going on, to notice what's not being said, and I do that much better when I'm quiet."
- **The Pre-Frame for Claiming Work**: To get credit without bragging, describe the difference the work made instead of saying "I did that." Open with a line like "One of the things I found when working on this piece was…" That flags your contribution and adds to the discussion at the same time.
- **Battery Management and Brilliant Battery Boosters**: Watch your energy level closely, and check your calendar ahead so you can pre-charge before draining events and schedule recharge time after them. Start meetings at 10 past the hour to create buffers. Keep quick boosters on hand: acupressure points, citrus essential oils, moving between meetings, a mindful pause at doorways, and breathing with a longer exhale than inhale.
