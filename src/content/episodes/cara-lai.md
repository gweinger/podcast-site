## Key Stories
- **The 2 a.m. anxiety loop**: Greg opens by sharing a fresh flare-up of panic and how radical acceptance — not control — is what finally pulls him through, setting the stakes for Cara's whole approach.
- **From climate activism to compassion**: Cara describes punishing herself with cold showers and denial in the name of climate action, then realizing on retreat that fear-driven activism only adds more fear to the world.
- **Social anxiety in an extroverted culture**: She recounts arriving at meditation as a quiet introvert who never quite fit, and how practice let her stop morphing herself to look like she belonged.
- **Letting your face fall off**: On a silent retreat, Cara noticed she'd been holding an "I'm fine, don't look at me" expression for who knows how long — and the muscular release of dropping it became the seed of her class "Meditate Your Face Off."
- **The finance guy in the crisis town hall**: Greg recalls a quiet executive who took over after a CEO scandal and led with honesty about what he knew, didn't know, and felt — turning the company around by being human instead of polished.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Meet fear with kind attention**: Instead of controlling or suppressing anxiety, turn toward the fear with curiosity and compassion; the felt sense of being cared for is what actually loosens its grip.
- **Shift from doing to receiving**: In any moment — meditation, brushing your teeth, a hard conversation — widen attention to sensations, sounds, and sight coming in, rather than the planning, defending, or performing going out.
- **Practice at the emotional flashpoints**: Use the moments you're most triggered (traffic, a recurring fight, childcare overwhelm) as your practice. Before reacting, ask, "What am I actually feeling right now?"
- **Start with eight minutes (or less)**: Cara built her practice on eight minutes a day from a book of the same name. Three or four minutes is fine — consistency matters more than duration.
- **Yin/yang as a leadership diagnostic**: Yang is active, doing, moving things. Yin is receptive, feeling, listening. Cultures over-index on yang; leaders who add real yin — listening without a canned answer — bring more attuned, less blind action.
