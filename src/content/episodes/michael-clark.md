## Key Stories
- **Puking in the hotel room before LA pitch meetings**: While peers gathered in the lobby, Clark was upstairs nauseous, sweating and gagging. The stage fright was so physical it felt like trauma. Years later, the same writers and producers asked Clark to teach them how to pitch.
- **Church acting as the turning point**: Acting in a small church group worked out the nerves and stage fright, and that confidence carried straight into pitch meetings.
- **The glasses that switch Clark "on"**: Clark's glasses are functional, not fashion, but they act as the cue that turns on the presenting self. Clients find their own version, such as a shirt, a blouse, a way of sitting, or their set.
- **The jewelry that "only took five minutes"**: Clark's wife wanted to underprice her jewelry because it came easily to her. Clark pushed back that price reflects materials, comparables and the result, not effort.
- **Wilt Chamberlain's pre-game ritual**: Greg shared that Chamberlain's teammates worried when he *didn't* throw up before games. Nerves can be a sign you're about to perform.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Action / Cut**: Borrowed from film sets. You rehearse as yourself, step into the role when the "director" calls action (the meeting, the stage, the Zoom), then recharge as yourself when it's over. The goal isn't to become an extrovert. It's to turn the light switch on and off comfortably.
- **Command the room internally and externally**: Externally, open with energy in the first four to five seconds ("Hey, good morning, Greg. How you doing today?") and set a relational agenda, not a slide count. Internally, walk in knowing you belong and were selected for a reason, including when you're the only one who looks like you in the room.
- **The four-stage practice ladder**: Write the pitch word for word. Rehearse it alone to inanimate objects (phone, camera, car). Then rehearse with friends and family before it ever reaches an investor or VP. Ask "Is there anything you would change?" instead of "Was it good?" Record yourself to check body language.
- **Practice the yes**: Football teams don't practice fumbles. End every role play with a yes so that hearing yes feels normal, which lifts your energy at the start of the real meeting. When a no comes, move on to the next play.
- **Brag bank**: Talk through past wins out loud with a coach, then turn the recording into a narrative of your successes. "Success leaves clues", and the record counters a brain that says you're not good enough.
- **Find the real "why" for energy**: Keep asking "What do you really want?" until you reach the core desire, such as a promotion, a raise or a new house. That genuine stake is the source of authentic energy, not volume or projection.
