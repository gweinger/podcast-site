## Key Stories
- **The Broadway auditions she walked away from**: Trained as a ballerina, Tamara moved toward roles that required singing, rehearsed everything perfectly with a coach, then buckled in the room every time — and concluded the work simply wasn't for her.
- **The panic attack on set**: Mid-broadcast her whole body started shaking and she couldn't speak; she thought she was having a seizure or a stroke. A doctor named it a panic attack, which sent her to a therapist and started the rewiring work.
- **Meeting Dan Harris**: A friend connected her to Harris, who had his own on-air panic attack. He gave her a signed copy of his book, and reading it landed as proof she wasn't alone or broken.
- **The calendar audit in March**: At a conference, a speaker said if your top priority isn't on today's calendar, you'll never hit it. Tamara looked back over a month of investor calls and conference days and found zero calls with her number one priority — customers.
- **Leaving the news without a plan**: She framed the pivot as a small failure at the time — she hadn't landed the job she wanted at the top network — and now describes the space it opened as the thing she'd choose again.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Rewrite the scenario**: Write down the worst-case version your brain is rehearsing, then rewrite the same event in the best possible outcome and say it aloud. Seeing the two versions side by side exposes how little the anxious script matches the room you're actually walking into.
- **Box breathing, in public**: In four, hold four, out four, hold four, counted silently. Tamara uses it on site and before speaking; it's the nervous-system reset she pairs with cold plunges.
- **Daily ideal outcomes**: Ten to twenty minutes of meditation plus a written statement of the ideal outcome for the day — including for a specific talk — so the day gets run rather than doing the running.
- **Written annual goals you carry**: Roughly five company goals plus a few personal and health goals, written down, kept in a physical planner, and reviewed daily as a North Star rather than posted where they blur into noise.
- **Calendar-to-priority alignment check**: Name your number one priority, then scan the last 30 days of calendar. If nothing on it serves that priority, stop and fix the calendar before doing anything else.
- **Collaborative-then-decisive leadership**: Gather the team's thinking fully, then step up and close the decision. Tamara frames the alternative — authoritarian speed — as counterintuitively slower because it strands the intelligence you hired.
