## Key Stories
- **The only introvert in sales**: Leadership told her she needed to be bubbly and charismatic to succeed. She was successful the whole time, and still spent years wondering whether she was in the wrong career instead of recognizing what her quietness gave her.
- **Yoga as the only safe way back into the body**: In treatment for an eating disorder in her twenties, yoga was the one physical practice that felt safe. She hadn't realized how disconnected from her body she'd become or how hard it was to name what she felt.
- **Teaching yoga the wrong way on purpose**: She never cared about students' alignment — if it felt good in your body, it was right for your body. Students started asking about the internal, emotional side, which spiraled into coaching clients and eventually the business.
- **Education before exercises**: Early on she jumped straight into somatic work with clients and it didn't take. The brain wants top-down understanding first, so now she starts with neuro-education about what's physiologically happening.
- **The feedback loop at work**: Her worked example — someone who reacts to daily feedback as "they're on my back, they don't think I'm doing my job" — traces the reaction down through perfectionism to an "I'm not good enough" wound, then rebuilds it as an adult-sized interpretation.
- **She still sees a coach for her own nervous system**: Karen doesn't do this work alone, and says isolation is not regulation.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **80% travels body to brain**: One large nerve runs brain-to-body on a loop, but the great majority of communication starts in the body. If you're disconnected from physical sensation and trying to control everything cognitively, you're ignoring most of the signal.
- **The stoplight — note Karen's non-standard mapping**: **Red** = shutdown, freeze, lethargy, low energy, depression. **Yellow** = the regulated middle — calm, connected, curious, creative, courageous. **Green** = hyper-excitable go-go-go, busy, productive, in control. All three serve a purpose; the problem is getting stuck in one chronically. Karen sees many people run green all workday and crash into red at home. (Greg flags mid-episode that he'd had red and green backwards.)
- **In-the-moment down-regulation**: Feet on the floor. Deeper breaths. A tactile anchor — a blanket, your own legs. Pick one object in the room and study its colors. All of it signals safety and pulls you out of sympathetic activation.
- **Vagus nerve ear work**: Massage the earlobe, gently pull down, small circles; or press a fingertip into the spiral of the outer ear where you feel a hard ridge and massage there. Karen's preferred quick reset toward ventral vagal state.
- **Ask for the pause, and use an excuse if you need one**: "I hear what you're saying — give me five minutes, I need to get a drink of water." On a call: "Can you hold on a second?" Karen is explicit that it's fine if it isn't strictly true, and that they may not react well — their reaction isn't yours to carry.
- **Notice where you're speaking from**: Before you respond, check whether you're communicating out of shutdown, overreaction, or curiosity. You won't be perfect; awareness is the point.
- **Widen the window**: The regulated middle is a capacity that can be expanded with practice, not a fixed trait.
- **Intercept the pathway**: Because the reaction hits the body before the mind, catching it early stops you reinforcing the neural pathway you've spent years building. Substitute a different response and eventually the new pathway becomes automatic.
- **Protector parts**: Perfectionism, hypervigilance, and control developed to protect you and mostly did their job in childhood. They rarely help in adulthood. The goal isn't to delete them — it's to stop them driving.
- **Titrate, and don't do the deep work alone**: Moving too fast is itself damaging to the nervous system. Co-regulation with someone steady is part of the mechanism, so find a practitioner you feel safe enough with to be raw and honest.
