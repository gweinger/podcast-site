## Key Stories
- **Pantyhose, pearls, and golf**: As a young immigrant in a formal 1990s corporate culture, Nikki dressed far older than her age and took up golf, which she hated, because it seemed to be what business people did. She realized it was turning her into someone even she didn't recognize.
- **Forced into the open**: In 2008 her partner died by suicide in their home, and the loss became public news. Nikki had not yet come out as gay. With no way left to hide, she chose to stop hiding, and she says the experience was freeing in some ways.
- **Laughed off the stage**: At a high school speaking competition in India she froze at the microphone in front of about 1,000 students. The silence turned into laughter across the whole auditorium, and a teacher had to lead her off the stage by the hand. That day she decided she would one day be paid to speak on stages around the world.
- **Not Tony Robbins**: She tried to copy the big, theatrical style of speakers like Tony Robbins and found the results "frankly mediocre." Once she let go of that belief and spoke in her own style, more people related to her.
- **The wartime CEO**: Her peers call her a wartime CEO because she gets calmer in a crisis. Her teams say her calm presence gives them confidence that they can make it through.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Find your zone of genius (Ikigai-style)**: List what comes effortlessly to you but takes effort for others, what absorbs you until you lose track of time, and what you are endlessly curious about. Ask the people around you what they rely on you for. Match that to market demand, double down on it, and use AI for the parts you aren't designed for.
- **Get the reps, then find your style**: Say yes to every speaking opportunity, "even if there were three people in a room with a pulse." Volume builds skill, and an authentic style emerges once you stop copying someone else's persona.
- **Abundance framing for audiences**: There are eight billion people on the planet. Instead of trying to please everyone, assume someone relates to how you are designed, speak to them, and stop chasing audiences that aren't yours.
- **Reinvent repeatedly, and build with your own hands**: Each AI model update changes the landscape, so adaptability has to be ongoing. Senior leaders should move back toward execution and building, because you understand AI only by working with it, not by being briefed on it.
- **Orchestrator, not traffic cop**: As management roles that only supervise others shrink, position yourself as the person who provides context, orchestrates outcomes, and creates value.
