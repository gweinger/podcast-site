## Key Stories
- **A secret introvert in a loud industry**: Alexson felt out of place among the outgoing publicist stereotypes of PR. She found that the job, finding mutual benefit and building trust, rewards introvert strengths like observation, pattern-spotting and one-on-one rapport.
- **A role model who didn't apologize**: Her leader at the American Heart Association, Toya Honoré, treated her own introversion as a strength. Seeing that showed Alexson she didn't have to be like everybody else in the room to lead.
- **Leaving during lockdown**: The stress of national PR work while schooling her kids at home during COVID pushed her to walk away and start her own firm. She then had to become something she had never been before: a visible introvert.
- **Walking into a networking event and walking right out**: Alexson once treated networking as a dirty word. Now she brings a buddy and arrives prepared. She also gives herself permission to turn around and leave when the vibe is wrong.
- **"I probably won't watch this podcast interview"**: She still dislikes watching herself on camera. That is why she likes AI practice tools, which give impersonal feedback that nobody else ever sees.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Talk about the work, not yourself**: Shift from "look at me, look what I did" to "look what we built, look what's happening because of the small part I played." Passion for the work makes you more compelling and helps you get past the discomfort of speaking up.
- **Message library**: Write down your key messages, the statistics and stories that prove them, and what you want people to do. Refine them privately, then practice them out loud until they are muscle memory, so they hold up when you freeze in front of a microphone, a camera or a room.
- **Low-stakes to high-stakes practice**: Start with private reflection and writing, then say your messages aloud with a trusted partner. After that, record yourself and use AI tools to analyze pauses, inflection and filler words like "like," so you can improve without anyone judging you.
- **Bridge back to your key messages**: Prepare for unexpected questions by anticipating them and knowing what you are there to say. When a question catches you off guard, connect it to a point you came to make: "Here's what I do know."
- **Podcast pitching built on mutual benefit**: Start with your goal, find the shows your people already listen to, and pitch the value you bring to that audience rather than what you are selling. Don't skip small shows, because the trust listeners place in a host means even small numbers can lead to big results.
- **Half a step outside your comfort zone**: Take small, deliberate moves toward visibility, such as a social account about your business or mission, or one networking event with a buddy and a prepared closing question: "Is there any way I can help support your business?"
