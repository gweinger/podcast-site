## Key Stories
- **From intern to executive by combining deep analysis with clear delivery**: As a young quantitative analyst, Sebastien prepared seriously for a research presentation and delivered it clearly. Once his bosses realized the deep quant work came packaged with strong communication, he was hired and quickly asked to explain difficult concepts to clients.
- **The human piranha on steroids**: Sebastien pairs with an extroverted salesperson colleague on client trips — the salesperson runs small talk, Sebastien delivers expertise. A working example of complementary strengths, and a template he now sees across industries.
- **Rockefeller and the cost of anger**: Sebastien recounts a moment from Rockefeller's biography where, under attack, Rockefeller stayed quiet while his team clamored to fight back — then told them, "You have to realize that anger is expensive." A study in when silence is power.
- **The 80/20 media interview**: After feeling talked over in a joint media interview, Sebastien fed the published transcript into AI and confirmed his colleague had taken 80% of the airtime. Data-backing what his gut already knew.
- **The Muhammad Ali contrast**: Greg brings in the Ali biopic — long silent training scenes cut with the witty, charismatic press-conference Ali — as a visual of how public performance is birthed in private reflection.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **The One-Over-N Rule**: In a meeting of N people, each person should contribute roughly 1/N of the airtime. Sebastien names it openly in his steering committees so people self-regulate — dominators catch themselves; quiet contributors know they're expected to step up.
- **The Pivot Method**: When someone monopolizes, the leader (1) politely interrupts, (2) briefly summarizes what they said so they feel heard, then (3) pivots to a quieter person — often by reading body language or knowing who's an expert on the topic.
- **"How much time do we have to make this decision?"**: Sebastien's opening question for any decision on his desk. It exposes false urgency, preserves optionality, and lets strategic patience do its work — especially useful when a team is trying to hand you the monkey.
- **Deliberate non-resolution of team disagreements**: Instead of reflexively resolving every John-vs-Mary dispute, leave some conflicts unresolved. Teams often figure it out themselves, and you stop being the bottleneck for every decision.
- **Speak 50% less than what's in your head**: For introverts in fast meetings, filter down to the most worthy point rather than trying to make every point. The signal-to-noise ratio of your contribution rises.
