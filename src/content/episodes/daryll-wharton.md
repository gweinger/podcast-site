## Key Stories
- **674 pounds and 25 rounds of Weight Watchers**: Daryll joined Weight Watchers with their mother at age seven and rejoined at least 25 times. Weight loss surgery brought the weight down to the high 300s, where it stalled for years.
- **Mad at The Secret, then trying it anyway**: After watching The Secret, Daryll said, "You guys have got to be kidding me." Daryll still started saying one affirmation they didn't believe, "I am disciplined and focused." Soon came 4:30 AM sessions with a trainer and a plant-based diet. The words are now tattooed on Daryll's wrist.
- **Already a hypnotist, just undocumented**: During formal hypnosis training, Daryll realized the affirmation practice that had driven the transformation was hypnosis all along. Greg called it "patient number one."
- **Bangs at 60 over a "big forehead"**: Daryll describes clients who discover that a lifelong habit, like wearing bangs into their 60s, traces back to one offhand comment from a parent or friend.
- **Gratitude for the 674-pound self**: Daryll credits their past self, who chose surgery at 28 despite fear and no prior hospital stays, as the reason for every change since. That gratitude shapes how Daryll makes decisions today for the "five-year-down-the-line me."

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Give your subconscious a nickname**: Name your subconscious mind so you can treat it like a person you'd help without question. This creates enough distance to start supporting yourself the way you already support everyone else.
- **The two questions**: When you hear yourself say "I've always been this way," ask "When did I decide that?" and then "Why can't I change? Why not?" These questions expose borrowed beliefs and open space to choose a new one.
- **Throw a monkey wrench in the loop**: The brain loves a loop. Interrupt it with a small physical change, such as brushing your teeth with your other hand, while you hold the new feeling or goal in mind.
- **Plan the transformation like a vacation**: Script how you want your days to go instead of only wishing. Say affirmations on waking and at every red light. Set a mid-day alarm, and at 3:00 each day read a letter to your future self written as if every goal has already been reached.
- **Words, not sentences, plus one daily statement**: Shrink long affirmations into three to five identity words (e.g., grateful, worthy, abundant). Pair them with "Every day in every way, I get better," said like you mean it. Recording your own voice and listening at night builds approval from yourself first.
