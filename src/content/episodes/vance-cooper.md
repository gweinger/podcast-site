## Key Stories
- **The mid-30s turning point**: Through his mid-20s and into his PhD program around 2020, Vance quietly assumed something was wrong with him because he wasn't the handshake-everyone type. It wasn't until his mid-30s, studying leaders who succeeded without being the loudest voice, that he gave himself permission to lean into his supportive, empowering style.
- **Studying leadership vs. becoming a leader**: Vance realized during his doctoral work that you can study every leadership theory and still not know how to lead — because leadership is fundamentally a self-discovery process, not an academic one.
- **The Myles Munroe influence**: The late Dr. Myles Munroe was the first thinker who reframed leadership for Vance as self-discovery rather than traits, changing how he approached the entire field.
- **Leading outside the job title**: Without a fancy corporate title, Vance built leadership experience by becoming president of a nonprofit and serving on boards and advisory committees — proving that leadership opportunity doesn't have to come from your employer.
- **The introvert breaking point**: Vance describes the pattern of quietly tolerating a bad process for months, saying nothing, until the pressure explodes — and how that silence quietly erodes both authenticity and confidence.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Vision, Character, Attitude**: Vance's three lenses for authentic leadership. Vision — can you articulate where you're going clearly enough that people want to follow? Character — do people trust you? Attitude — how you respond when things go wrong will determine your results.
- **Reverse-engineer the vision on paper**: Don't keep your vision in your head. Write goals on poster boards or paper you can see daily, then reverse-engineer the small wins and steps required to get there.
- **Commitment-based routine**: Build a daily routine anchored to the commitments you've made to yourself. Once the vision is set, "the commitment is the commitment" — protect it against the small things that quietly pull you off course.
- **Use an outside voice**: Work with a coach, mentor, or consultant who can ask the questions you wouldn't think to ask yourself. Introverts especially benefit from externalizing thinking that would otherwise stay locked in their head.
- **Create your own leadership lab**: If your job isn't giving you room to lead, look to nonprofits, boards, volunteer roles, or community initiatives. Don't wait for a title — build the experience where the gap already exists.
