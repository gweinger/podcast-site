## Key Stories
- **Losing his voice on stage**: Salvatore's first time on a stage ended in his voice failing him and him leaving in "terror and humiliation" — the catalyst for a 20-year study of the body, neuroscience, and psychology of communication.
- **The chief data officer who wouldn't share her thinking**: A brilliant client would listen silently through meetings and then drop the answer, leaving everyone struggling to catch up. Learning to echo what she heard and share her reasoning changed how she worked with people from then on.
- **Naming the part of himself he tried to hide**: Salvatore describes the energy it took to keep a "too ugly to be seen" part of himself hidden on stage — and how naming it, inviting it in, and letting it sit in the front row freed him to actually shine.
- **Meeting Perry the Perfectionist**: After reading Carol Dweck's *Mindset*, Salvatore named his inner perfectionist "Perry" and reframed every mistake as "okay, but what did I learn?" — the first time in his life he stopped letting perfectionism take him down.
- **Coaching two advisors to $200M**: The humble brag Salvatore uses as a live demo — he coached two financial advisors launching a capital fund who raised $200 million, $100 million more than they expected.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Share your thinking, don't just deliver the answer**: Introverts do the cognitive work internally and then hand people the clean conclusion. Walk the room through your reasoning first — people want to feel validated for their contribution, and they'll follow the answer more readily when they've seen how you got there.
- **Three-step imposter syndrome move**: (1) Name it to tame it — labeling the feeling shifts the brain from emotional to rational processing. (2) Regulate — use breath, grounding, tapping, or clenched fists to get back into the body. (3) Take one small action — not a four-point plan, just the very next step (smile, move, put your pen down) to get energy moving.
- **HIPP framework for impromptu speaking**: Headline → Insight → Picture → Point. Open with a statement, add your reasoning, paint a multi-sensory picture, then land the point (often echoing the headline). Salvatore built the muscle memory by running the framework on a random object every morning between brushing his teeth and his coffee.
- **The Presence practice**: Don't try to be perfect, be present. Become self-aware of where you withdraw or escape into the brain, then notice the gap between stimulus and response — how much space you give yourself before answering shapes the impact you have.
- **The Humble Brag formula**: "I took this action on this project and got this outcome." Stick to data, not opinions. Focus on impact, not process. Make it measurable. Deliver it in a neutral voice. Build up 10 you can drop into meetings when relevant.
- **Primacy, Recency, Frequency**: The first thing you say lands hard, the last thing you say lands hardest, and the thing you repeat most is what echoes in the audience's mind — so build a refrain into every talk.
