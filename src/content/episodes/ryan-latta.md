## Key Stories
- **"You're not an introvert"**: Ryan's wife doubted he was an introvert because he seems so different at work. His reply was that the outgoing version is just the job, which is why he calls himself a practiced introvert.
- **The AI consulting partner**: Ryan had Claude read years of Obsidian notes about his conversations and interview him about his weak spots. Each morning he types "It's a new day," and it tells him who to check in with.
- **Kicking out the director**: Other consultants warned that a team could never change. Ryan mapped the real relationships and found that everyone dreaded working with their director. He asked the director to skip the kickoff, and when the director showed up anyway, he asked them to leave. The transformation then worked.
- **Opening with the migraine**: Ryan began a meeting with VPs and executives by saying he had a migraine and wouldn't be at his best. Another consultant said he should never admit that. Ryan disagreed, and the group respected him for it.
- **Bomb emoji risk reports**: Ryan skipped his consultancy's two-week slide-polishing service. Instead he used plain black-and-white slides that rated each project risk with bomb emojis. The CTO of a household-name company told him it was the first report that showed him what the problems were.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Rapport mapping (from Esther Derby)**: Put yourself at the center of a web and mark each relationship as good or bad. Repeat the process for each person around you. Then trace the most favorable path to the decision-maker, choosing who to meet first and whether you need a sit-down or a good word.
- **Find the trusted advisor, not the title**: Every leader has someone below their level they turn to and ask, "What do you think about that?" On teams, find the person everyone quietly follows. Winning them over can make buy-in from the tech lead or architect unnecessary.
- **AI-assisted relationship upkeep**: Keep dated bullet-point notes on every conversation. Have an AI interview you about how you work and what you're bad at, then build a daily prompt that suggests who to reach out to.
- **Budget your discomfort**: Assume anything meaningful will cost you something uncomfortable. Plan for that cost in advance, avoid stacking expensive conversations on the same day, and keep recovery time clear after draining work like a four-hour workshop.
- **Push back on the plan**: When using the influence skill, ask the AI why it picked certain relationships and have it evaluate your own alternative plan. Notice the questions it raises about strained or missing relationships.
