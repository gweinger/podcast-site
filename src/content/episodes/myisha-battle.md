## Key Stories
- **Cindy Gallup's travel rider**: Myisha describes learning from sexology leader Cindy Gallup that she insists on a rest day before and after any big event. It reframed for Myisha how much recovery a visible, performative role actually requires — and now shapes how she books her own calendar.
- **The pandemic mirror**: Six months after moving in with her partner, lockdown forced him to reflect her overcommitted, "million things" energy back at her. Hearing "I don't like being around you when you're like this" pushed her back into therapy and started her real work on being different at home.
- **From four jobs to "time affluent"**: Early in her coaching career she worked four jobs and just powered through. Now she runs a full practice (coaching, writing, podcasting, group programs) but deliberately keeps her days spacious — and wants to pull back further.
- **The visibility trap of the girl-boss era**: When she launched her business in 2016, she absorbed the message that a female-led, sex-positive brand had to be constantly visible on Instagram to be taken seriously. After some success, she now posts sparingly and treats social media output as a limited energy budget.
- **The buffer commute**: A recurring intervention with clients — get off the train a stop early, walk the last blocks, or trade a brief hello with your partner for 15 minutes of quiet before you re-engage. A small buffer between work-self and home-self changes the quality of everything that follows.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Recovery days flanking visibility**: Treat any high-energy public event (talk, launch, back-to-back dates) like Cindy Gallup does — schedule a protected recovery day on either side rather than stacking obligations.
- **The work-to-home buffer**: Deliberately insert a transition ritual (walking, quiet time, a solo activity) between the professional day and partner/family time so you arrive present instead of flooded.
- **Crossover-skills audit**: Ask "how did you get to the place you are now in your career?" and port those same behaviors — preparation, proactive scheduling, follow-through, tolerating rejection — into dating and relationships, where most people have never trained.
- **Context over frequency (for sex and intimacy)**: Instead of chasing matched libido, identify the contexts (weekends, low-workload weeks, post-recovery mornings) where sexual energy is actually available, and design around those rather than shaming yourself for the times it isn't.
- **Quality-over-quantity dating**: Push back on the three-apps, hundred-dates advice. One thoughtful match is enough; canceling and rescheduling when depleted is better than a "quasi-interesting date where you're about to fall asleep."
