## Key Stories
- **"Fine, I'll go first"**: The awkwardness of sitting in silence in meetings became unbearable, so she decided to fall on the sword and speak — a learned competency driven by her values around impact and connection, not by temperament.
- **Getting bit for speaking up**: Senior colleagues reacted badly when she raised things — some felt side-flanked or undermined, some worked from a "don't raise it unless you have the answer" rule, and some simply couldn't hold an idea that was 12 to 18 months out.
- **The visionary who was always right too early**: She'd flag how a software upgrade would hit customer experience a year out, get told the team wasn't worried about that yet, then watch the same leader raise it six months later. She learned to never say "I told you so" out loud.
- **Four days without fresh air in Vegas**: At a conference she moved between hall and hotel room through indoor plazas built to look like sky and water, and only realized on a Walgreens run that she hadn't been outside in four days.
- **Naming a podcast *The Unworthy Leader***: She chose the name to provoke either immediate recognition or disgust, because she'd spent her life feeling not good enough despite consistent achievement.
- **The near-death experience**: About two years before the interview, she came out the other side believing she was saved intentionally — and realized her greatest transgression had been the hours she spent worrying.
- **Burpees and the love experiment**: Mid-coaching-training, she swapped "I hate this" for "I love this" while doing burpees she genuinely hated, and the set vanished. It became her template for changing motivational fuel without changing output.

## Techniques & Frameworks
- **Flex within a reasonable comfort zone**: Personality is fixed; behavior flexes. Leadership is choosing what the moment needs in either direction, which turns "I have to perform extroversion" into "I have a choice."
- **The five timeless leadership behaviors**: Preparation, deliberateness, listening, precision when you do speak, and follow-through. All available to an introvert; none require dominance.
- **Honor the instinct**: The thought you swallow in a meeting is intuition doing its job. Kendra's rule after years of regret is that the discomfort of saying it is smaller than the cost of not.
- **Build consensus before the meeting**: Where you have the runway, align people in advance so you walk in as a united position rather than dropping a cold question into the room.
- **Devise a system instead of fixing yourself**: If insight reliably arrives 30 minutes after the meeting, don't pathologize it — send the email. "The conversation doesn't have to be over just because the meeting ended."
- **Planned extroversion plus recovery**: Kendra treats extroverted output as deliberate and budgets recovery around it — catnaps, fresh air, a shower, a corner of the room with her phone out, or converting a crowd into one conversation.
- **Name the benefit, then price the cost**: Every behavior you complain about is delivering something. Be honest about what fear-driven achievement buys you, then be equally honest about what it costs.
- **Love as motivational fuel**: Swap fear for love (or service, if love reads as too soft) and you often get the same result feeling markedly different afterward.
- **Separate worth from feedback**: Feedback tells you what to do differently; it says nothing about your worth. Conflating the two is where imposter syndrome lives.
